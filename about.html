About Finwell

Fifteen years of standing up for the ocean's most misunderstood animals.

Finwell Ocean Trust is an independent, science-led nonprofit working to end shark finning, reform destructive fishing practices and protect the marine ecosystems that more than three billion people rely on for food and livelihoods.

Our story

It started on a dock in Baja California Sur.

In the spring of 2010, marine biologist Dr. Elena Marsh was surveying hammerhead populations near the Sea of Cortez when she came across a fishing camp drying more than four hundred shark fins on its rooftops. The bodies had been thrown back into the water. Many of the sharks had still been alive.

Elena returned to Monterey determined to do more than publish another paper. Together with fisheries lawyer David Okafor and a small group of volunteers, she founded Finwell Ocean Trust in 2011 with a simple premise: sharks will only be protected when the people who share the water with them have a reason, and the means, to protect them.

“Remove the predators and the whole system unravels. Reef fish collapse, seagrass disappears, fisheries fail. Protecting sharks isn't charity for one species — it's maintenance for the entire ocean.” Dr. Elena Marsh, Founder & Chief Scientist

Our mission

We work to end the trade in shark fins, restore depleted shark and ray populations, and transition coastal fisheries toward practices that keep marine ecosystems productive for generations. We do this through three things we believe in: rigorous field science, enforceable policy, and partnerships with the fishing communities who know these waters best.

How we work

Our scientists tag and track sharks across the Eastern Pacific to map critical habitat. Our policy team uses that data to advocate for fin-trade bans, marine protected areas and bycatch limits at national and regional levels. And our Coastal Livelihoods program supports artisanal fishers in Mexico, Ecuador and Fiji as they move away from shark fishing toward sustainable, higher-value alternatives such as eco-tourism and selective gear.

Finwell is a registered 501(c)(3) organization. We do not accept funding that would compromise the independence of our research, and we publish audited financial statements every year.

Milestones

Where we've been

  1. 2011

    Finwell Ocean Trust is founded in Monterey, California, with three staff and a borrowed research skiff.

  2. 2013

    Our first satellite-tagging expedition maps hammerhead migration between Cocos Island and the Galápagos.

  3. 2016

    Launch of the Coastal Livelihoods program with 14 fishing cooperatives in Baja California Sur.

  4. 2019

    Finwell research is cited in the CITES Appendix II listing of mako sharks.

  5. 2022

    We help secure the expansion of the Hermandad Marine Reserve, protecting a key shark migration corridor.

  6. 2025

    The Finwell Fellowship funds its 100th early-career researcher from a coastal nation.
